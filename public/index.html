TurtleBlock AI

Constructivist AI agents for building, exploring, and iterating computational worlds in Minecraft and beyond.

Building in public.

WorldSpec is underway.

WorldSpec is the edition-independent representation layer between a learner's idea, a conversation with Turtle, and a world that can be built, inhabited, tested, reflected upon, and revised.

learner intent → agent dialogue → WorldSpec → build → inhabit → notice → reflect → revise ↺
Schema

What a world can represent: objects, relationships, form, function, materials, meaning, constraints, reflection, and history.

Lexicon

The shared vocabulary for making and thinking: create, move, scale, remix, preserve, test, inspect, reflect, fork, restore, and more.

Interaction grammar

Rules for when Turtle should execute directly, expose an interpretation, ask a question, preserve ambiguity, or invite reflection.

Not an AI world vending machine.

The learner remains the designer and producer. A concrete request can become a direct operation. A statement such as “make this place feel less authoritarian” should not silently become a universal architectural recipe. Turtle should help the learner articulate what that idea means here, make assumptions visible, and keep the resulting interpretation revisable.

Ideas become things to think with. Build them. Walk through them. Notice what happened. Change them.

Intellectual lineage

TurtleBlock AI grows from Dr. Bryan P. Sanders' work on Critical Techno Constructivism, STEAMHAMLET, Minecraft as a learning environment, purposeful play, and co-active emergence. That work emphasizes student voice, open-ended inquiry, collaborative making, multiple entry points, and computers as construction spaces rather than delivery machines.
