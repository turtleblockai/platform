## Prior Next Edge answered

The inherited public horizon asked:

> Can TurtleBlock AI support a shared world in which different collaborators keep distinct attention agendas—and sometimes choose to share or negotiate them—without collapsing everyone into one machine-selected foreground?

The September 15 contribution gives that question a first executable research representation rather than a finished product behavior.

## What the Plural Foreground Envelope adds

The schema represents a shared world and shared event while allowing at least two participants to retain distinct foreground states.

Each participant foreground can preserve:

- participant identity by reference;
- learner/collaborator actor type;
- human-interaction evidence provenance;
- privacy class;
- attention state such as `surface_now`, `defer`, `remain_silent`, `unconcerned`, or `unspecified`;
- a purpose reference;
- an optional attention-rule reference;
- selective disclosure at `private`, `existence_only`, `rule_without_rationale`, or `full` levels.

A shared rule separately records:

- whether it is absent, proposed, active, rejected, or withdrawn;
- who authored it;
- affected participants;
- consenting participants;
- purpose and trigger references;
- rationale visibility;
- a hard boundary that the rule cannot authorize new collection.

The decision layer can preserve plurality rather than force agreement through actions such as `remain_plural`, `surface_to_consenting`, `defer_shared`, or `no_shared_foreground`.

The provenance layer makes several research commitments explicit:

- private foregrounds remain preserved;
- sharing a world does not imply sharing an attention agenda;
- the machine has no consensus authority;
- the machine may not average participant priorities into a synthetic group priority;
- participants can withdraw;
- selective disclosure is supported;
- synthetic activity has no production authority;
- the envelope cannot expand surveillance;
- the arrangement remains reversible.

## What this does not yet establish

The September 15 commit adds the schema only. It does **not** yet add a hostile regression suite or deterministic validator for plural-foreground cases. Therefore the schema expresses the intended contract but has not yet earned the same executable adversarial coverage as the Learner Verification, World Evidence, and Selective Attention envelopes.

It also does not activate multi-user attention behavior in live Turtle sessions, Minecraft, TRY IT, or Turtle Terraria.

## Sanders ontology mapping

The immutable dissertation source layer remains unchanged. This is a later TurtleBlock research artifact.

Relevant mappings:

- **Freedom and Individuality** — a participant may retain a distinct foreground rather than inherit the group's or machine's priorities.
- **Shared Democracy** — collaborators may share a world while disagreeing about what deserves collective attention.
- **Problem Posing Education** — an event may become a shared question through human choice rather than machine declaration.
- **Social Impact** — deciding whose foreground becomes visible or interruptive is a consequential power choice.
- **Predetermined Outcomes** — the envelope resists turning shared computation into one compulsory machine-selected path.
- **Pedagogy** — surfacing, deferring, silence, disclosure, and negotiation all shape the learning environment even before content is delivered.

Later Sanders concepts:

- **Co-active Emergence** — several humans and Turtle may influence what emerges while retaining distinct provenance and authority.
- **Possible Possibles** — multiple legitimate directions can remain alive at the same time.
- **Purposeful Play** — collaborators may wander or attend differently without being synchronized into one imposed task.
- **STEAMHAMLET** — a shared room of possibilities can contain several simultaneous human purposes.
- **Cohabitation Without Provenance Collapse** — private foregrounds, shared rules, system proposals, and group decisions remain distinguishable.

## Seven established CTC operational domains

### Personal Inquiry
Primary. Each participant retains a purpose reference and an individual attention state.

### Compelling Problem or Question
Supportive. A shared event may become collectively compelling without requiring every participant to interpret it identically.

### Technology as Tool to Think With
Primary. Attention and disclosure become inspectable computational objects rather than opaque machine ranking.

### Formative Demonstration of Learning
Supportive. Changes in attention rules or disclosure may later document evolving inquiry, but this schema is not itself evidence that learning occurred.

### Reflection as Learning
Primary. Participants may surface or defer different events and later compare what each noticed without forcing synchronized reflection.

### Social and Cultural Critique
Primary. The schema exposes power questions about whose concerns are broadcast, whose stay private, and who can authorize a shared rule.

### Sharing and Collaborating
Primary. The contribution directly models collaboration without requiring consensus or complete disclosure.
