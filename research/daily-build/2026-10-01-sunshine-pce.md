## Chiseled problem

**How can a Persistent Computing Environment preserve enough of learning to support future return without letting its own choices about what becomes salient again turn continuity into a quietly curated curriculum?**

> **When a PCE can keep almost everything, who decides what becomes salient again?**

## Working findings

- **PERSISTENCE ≠ FOREGROUNDING.**
- A mature PCE should be able to preserve a trace without insisting on resurfacing it.
- **Quiet continuity** is a provisional term for keeping a trace returnable without making it continuously salient.
- **THE SLOW SIMMER IS PRESERVED POSSIBILITY WITHOUT COMPULSORY SALIENCE.**
- Retention, availability, resurfacing, intervention, and action may require distinct authority rules. This remains provisional.

“Class Never Ends” supplies a precursor in the broad preservation of classroom conversation as a “sea of text” from which learners can later select and recombine material. Siemens supplies the network pressure: maintained connections matter, but already-connected nodes can attract still more connections while weak ties can open routes to novelty. The PCE-side synthesis is inferential: longitudinal retrieval can compound salience unless persistence and foregrounding remain separately governable.

## TurtleBlock implementation relation

Selective Attention distinguishes observability, retention, salience, surfacing, and interruption. Initiative Authority permits quiet or responsive-only participation without erasing prior context. These are implementation/design evidence, not learner-outcome evidence.

> **TURTLE GUARDS THE SLOW SIMMER by preserving return paths without manufacturing urgency.**
