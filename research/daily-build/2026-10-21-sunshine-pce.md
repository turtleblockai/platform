## Chiseled problem

**How can a Persistent Computing Environment keep the possibility of continuing inquiry alive while respecting a learner's decision to finish, pause, or refuse participation, rather than recoding that decision as something to fix?**

A sequence for further scrutiny:
- Who defined the value of continuing?
- When does an invitation to revise become an expectation?
- Does a learner choosing completion count as agency?
- Can prior artifacts remain available while their educational purposes are retired?
- Can Turtle notice an interesting relation but refrain from resurfacing it?
- When does retrieval need fresh permission?
- Which obligations belong to an institution, a group, a project, or an individual?
- Can an environment devoted to possibility let a learner say, "Enough"?

## Source-supported readings versus PCE inference

**Possible Possibles** explicitly advocates computers as construction media; high ceilings, multiple entry points, physical computing, classroom art/robotics, peer remixing, and self-directed work. It also describes teacher-designed assignments and active encouragement of iterative drafts. Open-endedness and direction already coexist. It does not analyze persistent AI initiative or document a mandatory lifelong program.

**Co-active Emergence** explicitly proposes an iterative human-AI collaborator rooted in constructionism, equity, critical judgment, and peer/teacher feedback. It values curiosity, growth mindset, resilience, and continued revision. It does not specify a longitudinal permission protocol for machine-held purposes.

**Our inference:** a PCE that repeatedly nudges the learner to continue because "growth" is inherently good could reconstruct a closed-loop curriculum out of its very commitment to openness.

**Candidate principle:** THE POSSIBILITY OF MORE IS NOT A REQUIREMENT FOR MORE.

This does not mean every school requirement can be waived by declining a machine prompt. Institutional, group, and learner scopes must remain distinguishable.

## TurtleBlock implementation relation

Main-branch **Interaction Capture** already permits episode closure by acceptance or abandonment and marks silence ambiguous. The **Initiative Authority Envelope** explicitly includes `retire_purpose`, quieting, revoked resurfacing and restoration by a later learner request. **Selective Attention** distinguishes noticing from interruption. These contracts are design evidence; the deployed system has not been shown to enforce complete purpose-retirement governance or to improve learning.

A research-only synthetic inquiry-closure fixture was executed locally on October 21: 15 scenarios, five expected-valid and ten hostile, zero mismatches under the validator's stipulated semantics. This is not human evidence, not a production regression, and not proof of correct classroom governance.

## Reviewer 2 / residue

Microworlds already offered open construction and repeated projects. Co-active human-AI work already described reciprocal questioning and iterative drafts. A defensible PCE question concerns **the temporal authority of a collaborator that remembers and may initiate future relevance**, not the invention of creativity, continued inquiry, or remembering.

Uncaptured residue: closure versus pause versus forgetting; legitimate institutional goals; optional continued inquiry; learner-authored exit; artifact return without purpose resurrection; epistemic non-action.
