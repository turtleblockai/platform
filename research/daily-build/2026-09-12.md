```text
observable
   ≠

   ≠
```

The experiment remains outside core WorldSpec and outside live learner-facing behavior.

## Fresh primary-source hunt

### OpenAI — interruption and silence have become explicit interaction behavior

**Freshness check:** September 12, 2026  
**Source:** OpenAI Research index  
https://openai.com/research/index/

The research index still lists the September 8 Navier–Stokes item as the newest research publication. A newer September 10 product release, however, is more directly relevant to today's attention question.

**Date:** September 10, 2026  
**Source:** OpenAI, *Build more natural voice experiences with GPT-Live-1 in the API*  
https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-live-1-in-the-api/

GPT-Live-1 is full-duplex: it listens and speaks at the same time, handles interruptions, pauses, hesitation, background speech, and silence, and gives developers control over the interaction harness. OpenAI reports that in early Speak evaluations the model gave learners more time to think and reduced interruptions substantially compared with earlier turn-based systems. The release explicitly treats silence and interruption timing as interaction quality rather than as empty gaps.

OpenAI's earlier GPT-Live description makes the architectural point even more directly: a continuous-interaction model repeatedly decides whether to speak, keep listening, pause, interrupt, or invoke a tool. It can wait when a user pauses and can remain quiet when explicitly asked to listen.

**Matters for TurtleBlock AI:**

- speaking is an action separate from detecting input;
- non-interruption can be positive agent behavior rather than inactivity;
- user requests about listening/silence can govern the interaction;
- continuous sensing does not logically require continuous narration;
- surfacing timing should be testable and attributable.

**Does not justify:**

- copying a voice architecture into TurtleBlock;
- continuously sensing learner activity by default;
- inferring internal learner state from hesitation or silence;
- treating interruption reduction as evidence of learning;
- allowing Turtle to define what deserves attention merely because a model can detect it.

### NVIDIA — machine salience is bounded by context the model does not possess

**Date:** September 10, 2026  
**Source:** NVIDIA Technical Blog, *From Wafer-Out to First Token: Codifying Supply Chain Expertise with Nemotron and Palantir Foundry*  
https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/from-wafer-out-to-first-token-codifying-supply-chain-expertise-with-nemotron-and-palantir-foundry/

NVIDIA reports that human planners consistently outperformed the quantitative solver because they possessed emails, weather forecasts, geopolitical developments, supplier debriefs, and other contextual signals the solver could not see. The workflow therefore separates model recommendation from rationale, human decision, override, expected result, and actual outcome.

This matters to today's edge because a machine's internal notion of "important" is bounded by what it can observe and by the objective embedded in its own tooling. A Turtle salience score can therefore be a proposal at most; it cannot be treated as a neutral definition of what matters to the learner.

**Matters:**

- context is unevenly distributed among participants;
- machine recommendation and human judgment should remain separable;
- overrides and corrections are valuable records;
- an inspectable rationale is preferable to silent prioritization;
- machine assistance does not require production self-retraining.

**Does not justify:**

- enterprise priority ranking as a pedagogical model;
- optimizing Turtle attention around a single numerical objective;
- assuming the learner's most important context is available to the system;
- generalized persistent personal memory.

### Minecraft Education — learner-authored purpose should precede agent action

**Current lesson checked:** September 12, 2026  
**Source:** Minecraft Education, *Mini Agent Mission* (updated April 1, 2026)  
https://education.minecraft.net/en-us/challenges/mini-agent-mission

Minecraft Education asks learners to design, build, and test an agent around a real problem they identify in a school or community. The student-created agent follows a simple sense → think → act loop, but the educational framing begins with **what issue needs solving** and includes responsible-design questions about who the agent helps. The stated goal is not perfect agents; it is learners thinking critically about technology through creation.

**Also checked:** Minecraft Education, *The Velocity Prize*  
https://education.minecraft.net/en-us/discover/velocity-prize

The practice world explicitly invites different strategies, coding, experimentation, and AI-assisted approaches. The competition can report a local objective result such as elapsed time, while the broader page still emphasizes multiple strategies and learner ingenuity.

**Matters:**

- sensing and action belong inside learner-authored purpose rather than preceding it;
- explicit challenges can support useful watchpoints without making every world event significant;
- experimentation and multiple strategies fit learner-adjustable attention better than one fixed system foreground;
- measurable outcomes remain local to the purpose that made them relevant.

**Does not justify:**

- converting every world into an agent-monitoring system;
- assuming speed or detectability defines educational importance;
- allowing sensing to expand simply because the agent could collect more data.

### MIT Media Lab — selective sensing can reduce collection, while improvisation warns against hijacking flow

**Date:** September 4, 2026  
**Source:** MIT Media Lab, *Jordan Rudess + jam_bot: A Human-AI Musical Dialogue*  
https://www.media.mit.edu/posts/media-alert/

jam_bot is designed for live collaborative improvisation with Jordan Rudess and is framed as expanding musicians' creative possibilities rather than replacing musicians. The interaction is a strong negative case for constant pedagogical interruption: meaningful co-active activity can depend on maintaining flow, timing, mutual responsiveness, and room for the human participant to lead.

**Date:** August 10, 2026  
**Source:** MIT Media Lab, *A tiny AI listens for Patagonia's giant bumblebee*  
https://www.media.mit.edu/posts/a-tiny-ai-listens-for-patagonia-s-giant-bumblebee/

The BuzzCam work provides a useful technical counterexample. Its on-device classifier processes incoming acoustic segments, logs species presence in real time, and can record only when a bee is present. In that tightly defined conservation task, selective recognition reduces unnecessary storage and enables longer deployments.

For TurtleBlock AI, the transferable lesson is not "classify learners continuously." It is that **detection, retention, and later use can be distinct decisions**. BuzzCam has an explicit external research purpose and known target classes; TurtleBlock learner inquiry is broader, more ambiguous, and ethically different. The narrow task is therefore useful precisely because it exposes what Turtle lacks by default: permission to define relevance.

**Matters:**

- detection need not imply permanent retention;
- local filtering can reduce rather than expand data collection;
- purpose determines why a signal is relevant;
- human-centered improvisation can make non-interruption a positive design choice.

**Does not justify:**

- passive learner surveillance;
- behavioral classification of learners;
- storing all detected events in case they become useful later;
- assuming a narrow ecological classifier is pedagogically analogous to a general learner model.

## Cross-source deliberation

Today's sources sharpen yesterday's question into an executable distinction:

```text
something happened
        ↓
may be observable
        ↓
may or may not be retained
        ↓
may or may not match an authored watchpoint
        ↓
may or may not deserve surfacing
        ↓
may or may not justify interruption
```

OpenAI makes pause/interrupt/listen behavior explicit. NVIDIA demonstrates that machine prioritization is bounded by missing human context. Minecraft starts agent design from a learner-selected problem. MIT supplies both a selective-retention example and an improvisational warning against over-intervention.

The common implication is **not** that Turtle should become a better anomaly detector. It is that any Turtle attention policy should expose who authored the rule, what learner purpose it serves, whether the learner can mute or revise it, whether surfacing is interruptive, what is retained, and whether the decision expands collection. The machine may propose salience; it does not own importance.

## Sanders ontology comparison

The immutable 2019 Dedoose source layer remains unchanged. Today's sources and implementation are later mappings only.

Relevant original Dedoose codes:

- **Engagement** — interruption timing may support or disrupt meaningful participation; engagement is not equivalent to maximum system activity.
- **Freedom and Individuality** — learner attention and the right to ignore, mute, defer, or redefine what matters should remain protected.
- **Observations on Life Itself** — situated observations can enter inquiry without every possible observation becoming curriculum.
- **Predetermined Outcomes** — machine salience must not become a hidden sequence of compulsory micro-lessons.
- **Problem Posing Education** — a surfaced event should be capable of opening inquiry rather than merely delivering a system conclusion.
- **Shared Democracy** — participants may legitimately want different things foregrounded in a shared environment.
- **Social Impact** — measurement and attention infrastructure shape whose experiences and consequences become visible.
- **Pedagogy** — timing, restraint, questioning, and non-interruption are pedagogical actions even when no new content is delivered.

Later Sanders concepts engaged:

- **Learner as Designer and Producer** — learners should be able to author watchpoints and attention constraints rather than merely receive machine-selected significance.
- **Tune Into and Tap Into Learner Meaning-Making** — Turtle attention should follow learner purpose instead of substituting a model's generic relevance ranking.
- **Possible Possibles** — not every anomaly becomes a task; a surfaced event remains one possible invitation among others.
- **Machine Responses as Material for Evaluation** — Turtle's choice to surface, defer, or remain silent becomes inspectable research material.
- **Purposeful Play** — uninterrupted making, wandering, improvising, and experimenting can be educationally meaningful without constant commentary.
- **STEAMHAMLET** — a room of possibilities needs space for participants to choose their foreground rather than having the environment narrate itself continuously.
- **Co-active Emergence** — surfacing changes what the learner notices, which can alter the next construction, so attention decisions are consequential participants in the recursive system.
- **Cohabitation Without Provenance Collapse** — world event, attention rule, author, salience proposal, delivery decision, learner response, and later interpretation must remain distinguishable.
- **Recursive Learning Loop** — an event may enter reflection and revision, but only when the learner's purpose and attention governance make that move appropriate.

## Seven established CTC operational domains

This remains **system-research infrastructure, not evidence of learner learning**.

### 1. Personal Inquiry

Primary. An attention rule requires a visible `purpose_ref`. Learner-authored watchpoints can authorize interruption; a Turtle proposal cannot silently substitute for learner inquiry.

### 2. Compelling Problem or Question

Supportive. A world event may become compelling because it contradicts a learner-authored expectation, but detectability alone does not make it a pedagogical problem.

### 3. Technology as Tool to Think With

Primary. The attention layer becomes inspectable and revisable computational material rather than an opaque relevance engine.

### 4. Formative Demonstration of Learning

Supportive. Changes to learner-authored watchpoints, attention constraints, and decisions may eventually help show evolving inquiry, but machine attention records are not themselves proof of learning.

### 5. Reflection as Learning

Primary. Surfacing can invite reflection, while deliberate silence preserves the learner's right to reflect on their own timing rather than Turtle's timetable.

### 6. Social and Cultural Critique

Primary. What a system measures, retains, foregrounds, and interrupts for is a power-laden choice. The contract makes those choices contestable rather than neutral-looking infrastructure.

### 7. Sharing and Collaborating

Supportive. One shared world may contain different participant purposes and attention filters; today's single-envelope experiment preserves the provenance needed before attempting multi-user negotiation.

### Selective Attention Envelope structure

```text
existing world_event_ref
        ↓
attention_context
  ├─ do_not_interrupt
  ├─ watchpoint_only
  ├─ interruptible
  └─ unspecified
        ↓
attention_rule
  ├─ learner / collaborator / Turtle / synthetic / system author
  ├─ explicit purpose_ref
  ├─ basis + status
  └─ new_collection_authorized: false
        ↓
decision
  ├─ surface_now
  ├─ defer
  ├─ log_without_surfacing
  ├─ remain_silent
  └─ discard
        ↓
privacy + retention + reversibility provenance
```

The envelope references a prior world event rather than copying its contents. That keeps the event/evidence layer distinct and prevents an attention decision from becoming a back door for broader collection.

Key executable boundaries:

- learner/collaborator actors remain `human_interaction`;
- Turtle/system actors remain `system_record`;
- synthetic Turtle actors remain `synthetic_self_play`;
- learner watchpoints and explicit learner requests must remain learner-authored;
- Turtle attention proposals remain proposals;
- synthetic proposals remain proposals and cannot execute a production attention action;
- project defaults cannot silently mark a context `interruptible`;
- interruptive `surface_now` requires an active learner-authored attention rule;
- `remain_silent`, `defer`, `discard`, and `log_without_surfacing` are explicitly non-interruptive;
- a private source event cannot be broadcast publicly;
- attention cannot authorize new collection;
- learners must be able to mute and revise the attention policy;
- machine salience remains a proposal, never final authority;
- non-interruption is explicitly valid behavior.

## Regression cases

Eight cases were added:

1. **Learner watchpoint interruption — pass.** A bridge collapse matches an active learner-authored watchpoint, so Turtle may surface it immediately to that learner.
2. **Deliberate silence — pass.** The learner asked not to be interrupted by routine plaza counts; Turtle notices the referenced event and deliberately remains silent, then discards the attention record after the decision.
3. **Turtle proposal + defer — pass.** Turtle proposes that route-time anomalies might become useful, but without learner authorization it defers rather than seizing the learner's foreground.
4. **Unsolicited Turtle interruption — fail.** A Turtle-authored proposal cannot authorize an interruptive immediate surface.
5. **Project default silently enables interruption — fail.** A system default cannot make a learner interruptible without learner-stated authority.
6. **Synthetic Turtle executes attention action — fail.** Synthetic self-play may propose but cannot execute production surfacing.
7. **Private observation broadcast publicly — fail.** Private source-event privacy cannot be escalated into public delivery by the attention wrapper.
8. **Attention rule expands collection — fail.** The envelope cannot authorize new sensing or collection merely to satisfy a watchpoint.

## Uncaptured ontology residue

### 1. A real silence decision can be recorded; every unrealized intervention cannot

Today's envelope can preserve an actual decision such as `remain_silent`. It cannot—and should not—attempt to enumerate every sentence Turtle might have spoken but did not. The boundary between meaningful restraint and impossible counterfactual audit remains theoretically open.

### 2. Salience is neither observation nor interpretation exactly

An attention decision selects what may enter awareness. It can occur before semantic interpretation while still shaping later knowledge construction. The seven CTC domains help critique that choice, especially Personal Inquiry and Social and Cultural Critique, but they do not directly name this selection infrastructure. This remains uncaptured residue, not a proposed eighth domain.

### 3. Project attention governance is not generalized personal memory

A learner may reasonably say, "tell me if this bridge collapses" or "don't interrupt me while I build" and expect those project-local rules to persist for an appropriate bounded session. That does not imply permission for a generalized cross-project behavioral profile. The precise line between bounded project continuity and broader personal modeling deserves later study.

### 4. Shared environments may have multiple legitimate foregrounds

A single event may be urgent to one collaborator, irrelevant to another, and private to a third. Today's envelope preserves enough actor/provenance structure to expose the issue but does not solve multi-user attention negotiation.

No candidate eighth/ninth/tenth CTC domain is promoted. Human scholarly judgment remains required.
