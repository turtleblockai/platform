## Chiseled problem

**Can a Persistent Computing Environment finish the lesson without finishing the learner’s question?**

More precisely: how can a persistent computational partner complete a legitimate instructional task without treating that task’s successful completion—or the purpose that originally justified it—as authority over the learner’s continuing inquiry?

## Working findings

- **FADING HELP ≠ REVISING THE AIM.**
- **LOCAL MASTERY CAN SUPPORT OPEN INQUIRY.**
- Structured instruction, immediate feedback, prompting, and fading assistance do not by themselves establish learner authorship of purpose.
- Dewey’s treatment of educational aims adds a different requirement: aims should guide activity while remaining revisable as experience develops.
- A completed instructional objective may become material for later inquiry rather than proof that the larger inquiry is complete.
- Candidate principle: **A PCE SHOULD BE ABLE TO FINISH THE LESSON WITHOUT FINISHING THE LEARNER’S QUESTION.**

Skinner provides the historical pressure against weak novelty claims about adaptive support: programmed instruction already varied pace, supplied feedback and prompts, and could withdraw assistance as performance became more independent. Dewey provides the contrasting pressure on purpose: an educational aim should remain responsive to developing activity rather than act as an externally fixed endpoint.

## TurtleBlock implementation relation

Interaction Capture already distinguishes learner utterances, Turtle interpretations, actions, reactions, corrections, and later evidence. Existing authority envelopes also separate retention, salience, surfacing, and initiative. These are implementation/design evidence, not learner-outcome evidence.

The open implementation question is whether Turtle can distinguish:

- completion of an authorized instructional objective;
- revision or abandonment of the larger inquiry;
- expiry of permission to use the earlier objective as a reason for future nudges.
