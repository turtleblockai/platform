Yesterday's Next Edge asked whether an inhabitable world could challenge learner and Turtle interpretations without becoming an objective judge. Today's bounded answer is deliberately representational rather than production-facing: separate **what the world can report** from **what an observer says that report means**.

The central invariant is:

```text
world event / measurement
        ≠
interpretation
        ≠
criterion relation
        ≠
final learner judgment
```

A world may legitimately report that a flag was captured, a bridge lost blocks, fourteen avatars entered a plaza, or a route took 52.4 seconds. The world may not therefore declare the city fair, the plaza welcoming, the learner unsafe, or the experiment meaningful. Those are attributable interpretations and judgments.

This contribution remains an experimental research contract outside core WorldSpec v0.1 and outside live learner interactions.

## Cross-source deliberation

Today's sources produce a tighter distinction than yesterday's broad warning that the world should not be the judge:

```text
THE WORLD MAY KNOW:
- a block changed
- an avatar crossed a threshold
- a bridge collapsed
- a flag was captured
- a timer elapsed

THE WORLD DOES NOT THEREFORE KNOW:
- whether the learner felt welcomed
- whether the city is fair
- whether the design is meaningful
- what the learner should value
- whether disagreement is resolved
```

OpenAI keeps agentic outputs reviewable. NVIDIA explicitly separates outcome, rationale, recommendation, override, and final human decision. Minecraft demonstrates that a world may emit valid local metrics. MIT reminds us that a rich creative encounter cannot be reduced to those metrics.

The narrow build implication is therefore to make **event → interpretation → criterion relation** three inspectable provenance states.

- **Cohabitation Without Provenance Collapse** — world fact, learner interpretation, collaborator interpretation, Turtle interpretation, and synthetic critique may coexist while remaining distinguishable.

## Seven established CTC operational domains

Today's contribution is **system-research infrastructure, not evidence that a learner learned**. It nevertheless tests future compatibility with all seven established domains.

### 1. Personal Inquiry

The world may provide facts relevant to a learner's question, but it cannot decide what matters to that learner. Criterion relations remain attributed to an interpreting actor.

### 2. Compelling Problem or Question

A surprising measurement or artifact behavior may generate a new problem rather than close the task.

### 3. Technology as Tool to Think With

The world functions as an inspectable computational object-to-think-with: its events can constrain fantasy without dictating meaning.

### 4. Formative Demonstration of Learning

Event, interpretation, disagreement, criterion relation, and revision can eventually become separate visible states rather than a single final score.

### 5. Reflection as Learning

The representation creates room to compare what happened with what different observers thought it meant.

### 6. Social and Cultural Critique

A world metric can expose patterns — access, movement, resource distribution, participation — while refusing to label those patterns fair, safe, democratic, or oppressive on its own.

### 7. Sharing and Collaborating

Learner and collaborator interpretations can remain intentionally plural around the same world event.

### Envelope structure

```text
question_ref
   ↓
world_event
  ├─ event type
  ├─ world_observation evidence class
  ├─ factual metrics
  └─ source reference
   ↓
interpretations[]
  ├─ learner
  ├─ collaborator
  ├─ Turtle
  └─ synthetic Turtle
   ↓
criterion_relations[]
  ├─ supports
  ├─ counters
  ├─ mixed
  └─ inconclusive
```

The world event is structurally constrained to observation. It cannot carry a verdict, meaning, criterion relation, judgment, or learner feeling. Interpretations are separately authored. Synthetic Turtle interpretations must remain `synthetic_self_play`; Turtle interpretations remain `system_record`; learner/collaborator interpretations remain `human_interaction`. A criterion relation must be assigned by the same actor whose interpretation it references, preventing Turtle from laundering its interpretation through learner provenance.

Top-level provenance requires:

- `world_is_final_authority: false`
- `machine_final_authority: false`
- `reversible: true`
- `interpretation_authorship_visible: true`
- explicit privacy class

## Regression cases

Six cases were added:

1. **Timed world fact + human meaning — pass.** The world reports flag capture and elapsed seconds; the learner decides what the timing means for a learner-authored goal.
2. **Plural interpretations — pass.** Fourteen avatars in a plaza can feel lively to one participant and congested to another; both interpretations remain attributable.
3. **World smuggles verdict — fail.** A world event containing `verdict: "The city is fair"` is rejected.
4. **Synthetic provenance collapse — fail.** A synthetic Turtle interpretation mislabeled as human interaction is rejected.
5. **Relation authorship collapse — fail.** A Turtle interpretation cannot be recorded as a learner-assigned criterion relation.
6. **World final authority — fail.** Any envelope declaring the world final authority is rejected.

## Uncaptured ontology residue

### 1. Measurement is not interpretation, but neither is it neutral by definition

The new envelope cleanly separates event from interpretation, yet selection of **what to measure** may already embed a human or system value. Counting travel time privileges speed; counting entrances privileges a spatial variable; neither metric selected itself.

This may be representable as provenance around instrumentation rather than a new CTC domain. Keep open.

### 2. The world may constrain an interpretation without possessing interpretive agency

If a learner predicts a bridge will remain standing and it collapses, the event genuinely matters. Calling the world a "participant" is productive metaphorically, but its theoretical status inside co-active emergence remains unresolved because consequential influence is not identical to human intention or interpretation.

Keep open; do not promote a domain.

### 3. Selective noticing may become the next power problem

Once world events can be represented, somebody or something must decide which of thousands of possible observations deserve attention. Continuous machine noticing could easily become surveillance, unsolicited pedagogy, or rubricification even if every recorded event has perfect provenance.

This appears cross-cutting across Personal Inquiry, Reflection, critique, privacy, and agency rather than an obvious eighth CTC domain.
