## Sanders ontology comparison

The immutable dissertation Dedoose layer remains untouched. Today's external signals are later research inputs mapped onto, not inserted into, that source layer.

Relevant original dissertation codes include:

- **Problem Posing Education** — verification should open questions rather than enforce predetermined closure;
- **Freedom and Individuality** — the learner retains the right to challenge machine criteria and conclusions;
- **Predetermined Outcomes** — hidden evaluation criteria are a risk when they silently decide what counts as success;
- **Constructivism** and **Discovery Learning** — evidence can emerge through making, testing, inhabiting, breaking, and revising;
- **Shared Democracy** and **Social Impact** — criteria and claims should be available for collaborative challenge rather than hidden inside system authority.

Later Sanders concepts engaged:

- **Machine Responses as Material for Evaluation** — the machine's output is something to inspect and test;
- **Invisible Ideas Become Visible and Manipulable** — evidence criteria and uncertainty should become inspectable artifacts;
- **Possible Possibles** — verification can preserve competing hypotheses long enough to explore them;
- **Purposeful Play** — testing can happen through playful construction, breakage, repair, and explanation;
- **STEAMHAMLET / Make, Walk Around In, and Change the Story** — inhabitable construction can expose consequences that prose alone hides;
- **Co-active Emergence** — human criteria, machine interpretation, constructed evidence, and revision can change one another while preserving origin;
- **Cohabitation Without Provenance Collapse** — human, Turtle, synthetic self-play, world observation, and external research may coexist without becoming one undifferentiated voice.

## Seven established CTC operational domains

### 1. Personal Inquiry

A learner should be able to define or revise what evidence would satisfy their own inquiry. Hidden machine-generated success criteria would invert this domain by moving purpose back to the system.

### 2. Compelling Problem or Question

A failed test or counterexample should create a better question. Verification is useful when it resists premature closure.

### 3. Technology as Tool to Think With

Turtle, WorldSpec, code, tests, or Minecraft can make a hypothesis manipulable. Technology is part of thinking rather than merely the judge of an answer.

### 4. Formative Demonstration of Learning

The test, failed assumption, correction, revised criterion, and rebuilt artifact are all demonstrations of learning in motion. Only preserving the final pass state would destroy much of the evidence.

### 5. Reflection as Learning

A verification trace allows comparison among expectation, evidence, surprise, correction, and revised interpretation.

### 6. Social and Cultural Critique

Making criteria explicit makes them criticizable. Learners can ask whose assumptions or norms define correctness and whether the machine imported categories the learner did not choose.

### 7. Sharing and Collaborating

Shared evidence objects can support disagreement without collapsing authorship. Collaborators can compare criteria, evidence, and revisions rather than merely compare final answers.
