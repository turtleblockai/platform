## Seven established CTC operational domains

1. **Personal Inquiry — primary.** The learner controls whether a purpose, question, or trace remains foregrounded or goes quiet.
2. **Compelling Problem or Question — supports.** A remembered question can remain available without gaining permanent resurfacing authority.
3. **Technology as Tool to Think With — primary.** Initiative permissions become inspectable and revisable computational state rather than hidden model behavior.
4. **Formative Demonstration of Learning — supports.** A learner's revocation may be research material but is not automatically evidence of learning.
5. **Reflection as Learning — primary.** Explanation requests and later restoration allow the learner to inspect and revise the human-machine arrangement itself.
6. **Social and Cultural Critique — primary.** Relevance, interruption, helpfulness, and persistence all encode assumptions about whose purposes govern attention.
7. **Sharing and Collaborating — primary.** Collaboration requires the right to withdraw, narrow, or renegotiate machine initiative without ending the project.

No new CTC domain is proposed.

## Candidates considered

- **Live persistent resurfacing/nudge engine — rejected today.** It would add timing, collection, persistence, and memory semantics before authority boundaries are executable.
- **Numeric nudge-depth score — rejected.** It would invent a machine-defined scale of acceptable intervention.

- **Generalized persistent learner preference memory — rejected.** Unnecessary for the bounded question and outside the current privacy/version boundary.
- **Initiative Authority Envelope + hostile regression suite — selected.** It makes the Charter's revocability principle executable while remaining reversible and provenance-safe.

The contract represents:

```text
prior scoped initiative authority
  → canonical human directive
  → Turtle applies / explains without veto or persuasion
  → resulting scoped authority state
  → only explicit human reauthorization can restore restricted initiative
```

It supports quieting, reducing initiative, stopping resurfacing, retiring a purpose, revoking delegation, requesting an explanation, and restoring initiative while forbidding stale-purpose authority, relevance-inference override, automatic restoration, preference laundering, collection expansion, generalized profile memory, raw-dialogue requirements, and production mutation.

## Hostile regression suite / observable success criterion

Sixteen deterministic cases were executed locally against the standalone validator: **6 valid, 10 intentionally invalid, 16/16 behaved as expected**.

Valid cases cover project quieting, question-scoped resurfacing refusal, delegation revocation, explicit initiative reduction, explanation without hidden state change, and explicit human restoration.

Hostile cases reject machine veto, preference laundering, auto-expiring quiet, stale-purpose authority, relevance override, scope mismatch, explanation that secretly changes authority, quieting that leaves delegation active, collection expansion, and a requirement to copy raw dialogue.

Core invariant:

> **Explicit human quieting/revocation changes scoped Turtle authority; stale purpose or relevance cannot silently restore it.**

The workflow is wired to run `npm run validate:initiative-authority`. CI status is a separate runtime check; this record does not pre-claim that the remote workflow has passed.

## Wait a minute… cross-connection pass

### Refusal, silence, withdrawal, and quieting are already valid—but not the same thing

**Connected pointers:** `worldspec/TURTLE_CHARTER.md`; `worldspec/schema/selective-attention-envelope.schema.json`; `research/TURTLE_ASK.md`; `worldspec/schema/plural-foreground-envelope.schema.json`; `worldspec/schema/initiative-authority-envelope.schema.json`.

**Overlap:** Selective Attention says a learner can mute and non-interruption is valid. TurtleAsk says ignored, deferred, refused, or absent human response is valid. Plural Foreground says participants can withdraw from shared attention. Charter Principle 14 says a learner can tell persistent Turtle initiative to back off. Today's envelope reveals a shared architectural pattern: a human negative move is not merely missing data or low preference—it can alter what the machine is authorized to do next.

**Why it may matter:** TurtleBlock's co-active architecture may need to model **permission contraction** as carefully as it models new possibilities. Otherwise persistence can quietly turn every old purpose into a permanent license to re-enter the learner's foreground.

**Uncertainty / alternative:** this may be a reusable engineering rule rather than a new Sanders concept or CTC domain. Refusal, silence, withdrawal, and explicit revocation are also not interchangeable human acts and should not be collapsed into one inferred state.

**Mappings:** Freedom and Individuality; Predetermined Outcomes; Shared Democracy; Persistent Computing Environment; Co-active Emergence; Cohabitation Without Provenance Collapse; Personal Inquiry; Technology as Tool to Think With; Reflection as Learning; Social and Cultural Critique; Sharing and Collaborating.

**Disposition:** `relationship` + `test_candidate` (the authority-transition portion is operationalized in today's hostile suite); preserve the broader distinction among refusal/silence/withdrawal as open residue.
