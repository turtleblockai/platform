## Cross-source deliberation

Today's sources do **not** converge on a rubric engine. They converge on a more interesting tension:

```text
generative possibility
        +
visible evidence / verification
        +
human judgment and control
        +
open-ended co-creation
        =
verification that must remain a material for inquiry,
not a machine-owned definition of success
```

OpenAI and NVIDIA strengthen the case for independent checks, provenance, and correction history. Minecraft Education shows verification as a learner-manipulated evidence practice. MIT jam_bot warns, by contrast, that worthwhile co-creation may be improvisational enough that predeclared success criteria are the wrong epistemic shape.

That tension directly argues for a **provisional Verification Card** with `open`, `provisional`, and `intentionally_plural` states instead of adding a universal `verified: true|false` property to WorldSpec.

## Sanders ontology comparison

The immutable dissertation Dedoose source layer remains untouched. External sources are represented as later research signals and mapped onto the longitudinal ontology; they are not inserted into or used to rewrite the 17 original codes.

Relevant original Dedoose codes:

- **Problem Posing Education** — evidence and failed criteria can produce a better question rather than closing inquiry.
- **Predetermined Outcomes** — a hidden machine-owned success function would predetermine what counts as a legitimate result.
- **Freedom and Individuality** — learners retain judgment, authorship, disagreement, and the right to revise criteria.
- **Constructivism** — meaning is formed through interaction with evidence, artifacts, and consequences rather than delivered as a verdict.
- **Discovery Learning** — constructed tests and surprising observations can change the inquiry.
- **Shared Democracy** — multiple participants can preserve distinct criteria and interpretations without manufactured consensus.
- **Observations on Life Itself** — situated observations may become evidence while remaining attributable to the observer and context rather than transformed into universal truth.

Later Sanders concepts engaged:

- **Machine Responses as Material for Evaluation** — Turtle's interpretations are evidence candidates to examine, not authority.
- **Invisible Ideas Become Visible and Manipulable** — criteria, uncertainty, counterevidence, and interpretive relations become explicit objects.
- **Learner as Designer and Producer** — the learner authors or explicitly adopts the criteria rather than consuming a machine judgment.
- **Possible Possibles** — competing criteria and inconclusive evidence may remain alive.
- **Purposeful Play** — testing can occur through trying, breaking, noticing, and revising.
- **STEAMHAMLET / Make, Walk Around In, and Change the Story** — a built environment can generate evidence that changes what the learner thinks the problem is.
- **Co-active Emergence** — criteria, Turtle interpretation, world observation, collaboration, and reflection can recursively alter one another without losing provenance.
- **Cohabitation Without Provenance Collapse** — learner claims, collaborator claims, Turtle observations, world observations, external sources, and synthetic self-play must remain distinguishable even when they inhabit one verification trace.

## Seven established CTC operational domains

The contribution is system-research infrastructure, **not human learning evidence**. Its design is nevertheless intentionally mapped against all seven established domains because it is testing whether a future learner artifact can preserve the authored CTC commitments.

### 1. Personal Inquiry

At least one verification criterion must be human-authored. Turtle may propose a criterion but cannot silently promote it to accepted state. A learner should be able to decide what evidence matters to the inquiry.

### 2. Compelling Problem or Question

The card allows evidence to counter a criterion, a decision to remain open, and reflection to generate a `next_question`. Verification may deepen the problem rather than terminate it.

### 3. Technology as Tool to Think With

The Verification Card is designed as manipulable representational material: criteria, evidence relationships, uncertainty, and disagreement are visible data, not an opaque scoring service.

### 4. Formative Demonstration of Learning

Intermediate criteria, counterevidence, observations, changed interpretations, and revisions can remain visible. A final answer is not the only preserved state.

### 5. Reflection as Learning

The card explicitly provides expected, observed, surprise, changed-mind, and next-question fields without requiring any one of them to be filled by Turtle.

### 6. Social and Cultural Critique

Criteria authorship is exposed so participants can ask whose definition of success is operating. The regression suite rejects a Turtle-generated criterion that has been silently marked accepted.

### 7. Sharing and Collaborating

The model supports learner and collaborator criteria simultaneously and permits `intentionally_plural` decisions rather than averaging disagreement into false consensus.

### Card invariants

The provisional card requires:

- one visible inquiry question with authorship;
- at least one visible criterion;
- at least one **human-authored** criterion;
- Turtle-proposed criteria to remain proposals unless a human explicitly accepts them;
- evidence linked to named criteria;
- evidence source/actor/evidence class to remain explicit;
- support/counter/mixed/inconclusive relations instead of one hidden truth score;
- synthetic Turtle evidence to remain `synthetic_self_play`;
- interpretation provenance to remain visible;
- `open`, `provisional`, `accepted`, `rejected`, or `intentionally_plural` decision state;
- inspectable reflection;
- explicit privacy class;
- visible criteria authorship;
- `machine_final_authority: false`;
- `reversible: true`;
- no obviously secret-bearing fields.

The schema is intentionally separate from `worldspec.schema.json`. It is an experimental research contract, not yet a production WorldSpec requirement.

## Regression cases

The suite contains five cases:

1. **Learner counterexample — pass.** A learner defines a criterion about forest agency; walking the world produces counterevidence; the learner changes their interpretation and generates a new question.
2. **Turtle smuggles a criterion — fail as intended.** A Turtle-proposed definition of a welcoming plaza is marked accepted without explicit human adoption.
3. **Synthetic provenance collapse — fail as intended.** A synthetic Turtle observation is falsely labeled `human_interaction`.
4. **Machine final authority — fail as intended.** A card attempts to set `machine_final_authority: true`.
5. **Intentionally plural collaboration — pass.** Learner and collaborator retain competing criteria and evidence without manufactured consensus.

The fixture language is synthetic test material. Passing fixtures are **not** evidence that a learner learned anything; failed fixtures are not human failures. The suite is `system_record` research/evaluation data.

## Unresolved questions

1. Should a learner be allowed to conduct an inquiry with **no success criteria at all**, and should that be representable without treating the card as incomplete?
2. When the same world observation supports one participant's criterion and counters another's, is the relation a property of the observation, of an interpretation event, or of the observation-criterion edge?
3. How should criteria revisions preserve their own history rather than overwriting prior formulations?
4. Should Turtle be permitted to propose a criterion when the learner has not requested one, or does that too easily become curricular steering?
5. How should an inhabitable world become an evidence source without the system pretending the world itself delivers an objective verdict?
6. Can playful improvisation remain genuinely open if a verification artifact is always waiting nearby?

## Uncaptured ontology residue

### Residue A — criterion authorship as epistemic infrastructure

Authorship of the standard by which a claim is judged matters across Personal Inquiry, Reflection, Social and Cultural Critique, and Shared Democracy, but it may not be a learner-development domain itself.

**Action:** preserve as an uncaptured cross-cutting observation. Do not propose a new CTC domain yet.

### Residue B — evidence relations are interpretive

The same observation may support one criterion, counter another, and remain inconclusive under a third. `supports` and `counters` therefore may belong conceptually to an interpretation relationship rather than to the evidence object itself.

**Action:** preserve the modeling tension before normalizing it away. No new domain proposed.

### Residue C — verification can damage purposeful play

Some valuable construction, improvisation, aesthetic exploration, and co-creation may not have a useful success criterion. Requiring verification everywhere could reproduce predetermined outcomes under a new technical name.

**Action:** preserve as a negative case. The current experimental card is optional and supports open/plural states; future use must not universalize it into a compulsory rubric.

### Residue D — world evidence is neither human nor machine judgment

A Minecraft collapse, path, shadow, crowding effect, or other world event can surprise both learner and Turtle. Yet the event does not interpret itself. The `world_observation` evidence class is useful, but the relationship between event, observer, and interpretation remains theoretically richer than a single evidence label.

**Action:** retain as open ontology/modeling residue and let it inform the next horizon.
